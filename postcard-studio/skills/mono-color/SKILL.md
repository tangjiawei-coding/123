# 双色印刷

Project adaptation of yanliudesign/mono-color-skill, controlled two-ink mode. Original skill, design-system catalogs and MIT license are preserved in upstream/. Upstream example artwork is not bundled or reused. This prompt contains the runtime rules for the postcard format.

## Read and resolve

Identify one recognizable source subject, its mood, key identifying anchors and image role. Preserve identity and factual content while cropping, isolating, enlarging and converting it to mechanical print. Do not invent branded details or replace the subject. Select one composition around this specific photograph, not a fixed template. Make one final vertical 3:5 editorial postcard artwork, not a sheet of alternatives.

## Substrate and ink plates

Use a clean adaptive substrate: Neutral White #FAFAF7 for crisp imagery; Cool Gray #E9E9E5 for architecture; Pale Beige #F5F1E8 for intimate travel and tactile subjects. Never automatically yellow or distress paper.

Use exactly two printing inks with assigned roles. Default Cobalt #2148B8 + Terracotta #C65F38 for travel and leisure; Botanical Green #008A4B + Oxblood #8F3434 for plants; Charcoal #30343A + Signal Red #C83232 for architecture; Mint Green #5EB783 + Charcoal #302D2E for quiet observation. Choose the closest source-appropriate pair and keep it coherent. Dominant ink occupies 70–85% of printed area, accent ink 15–30%. Assign the dominant plate to the main photograph and the accent to selected subject details or title; do not scatter accent shapes. The substrate is not a third ink. Plate overlaps and halftone density are allowed, arbitrary extra colors are not.

## Reproduction and layout

The photograph becomes a printed plate: medium halftone screening, ink pooling and exposed-paper highlights. Maintain clear object contours. Use zero to two subtle controlled imperfections such as mild misregistration and uneven absorption. No digital duotone gradient wash, plastic painting, heavy grunge, antique props or distressed border.

Keep 25–55% visible empty paper, usually 35%, with 5–9% breathing margins. Use one strong focal event and one quiet release region. Choose an asymmetric image-led, specimen, journal or cover layout. One off-center crop or one oversized phrase supplies tension. Travel and leisure should feel relaxed. The hero image should remain large enough to read in a thumbnail; do not shrink it to an insignificant icon or fill whitespace with decorations.

## Type system

Use one short scene-derived English display phrase of 2–8 words unless the user supplies exact wording or requests no text. Preserve provided language. For intimate travel choose literary serif; for urban cultural subjects a clean grotesk; for structured content restrained narrow type. Use at most one secondary utility voice, only when actual supplied information needs it. Place type with deliberate alignment and meaningful contrast. Large type may overlap an uncluttered image edge but must not obscure a face or the main identifying shape. No fake dates, locations, numbers, logos, tiny invented editorial paragraphs or repeated microcopy.

## Visual check

Two inks only; source identity intact; exposed clean paper; actual print screening; one dominant focal event; readable concise type; balanced whitespace. No source-sample composition copying, mockup props, split before/after, device frame or extra postcard border.
