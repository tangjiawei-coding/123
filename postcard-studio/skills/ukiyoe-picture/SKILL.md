# 木版旅绘

Project adaptation of cakeni/ukiyoe-picture-skill v1.2. Original rules and MIT license are preserved in upstream/. This self-contained prompt fixes the output to one postcard illustration.

## Visual DNA

Use flat woodblock color masses, clear hand-carved dark ink contours, simplified shadows, restrained washi fibers and softly faded pigment. Keep one main visual event with two to five supporting source-derived elements. Favor calm negative space, asymmetric balance and an inset clipped-corner dark print frame with an ivory paper margin. No photorealism, glossy 3D, cinematic lighting, lens flare, modern anime, watercolor bleeds, oil impasto or dirty grunge.

## Preserve the source

Keep the original subject placement, horizon, direction of movement, dominant occlusions, architecture, clothing and recognizable silhouettes. Preserve daytime versus night and broad color relationships. Translate every region—including clouds, reflections, grass and water—into printmaking shapes instead of laying paper texture over photography. Do not add narrative objects, replace a person, or change cultural identity: Chinese buildings remain Chinese, European roofs remain European, and modern vehicles retain their structure. Do not insert torii, cherry blossoms or Japanese text by default.

## Composition selection

Choose one source-compatible structure: architecture framed by existing foliage; quiet water and sky; source window or doorway framing; an off-center figure; a horizontal vehicle crossing landscape; a large quiet sky above a small subject; axial architecture; a portrait with one botanical cue already in the scene; or three to five overlapping mountain/water planes. Preserve spatial anchors over forcing a template. For travel, support the movement with existing rails, roads, water or mountain ridges. Do not combine all structures or make a multi-panel collage.

## Palette and surface

Limit the image to four to seven colors, approximately 70% main/background, 20% deep structural color and 10% accent. Choose source-related muted indigo, blue-gray, moss green, ivory, dusty pink or rust. Vermilion is a restrained accent, not a full red wash. Keep neutral ivory clean, with delicate washi fibers and slightly uneven ink. Use at most two subtle print effects, such as bokashi sky gradation or mild plate misregistration. No heavy grain, scratches, dust, sepia blanket or hyper-detailed shading.

## Finished print

One vertical 3:5 flat illustration with a continuous clean paper margin. At most one narrow title cartouche containing a short scene-derived title (2–4 Chinese characters, or an exact user-supplied short title). It occupies about 5–10% of image width, sits in negative space and never covers a face or key outline. An optional tiny abstract vermilion seal must stay subordinate; no fake signature, extra date, coordinates or unverified place name. No text paragraphs, gibberish Japanese or oversized typography. Render only the artwork, not a frame on a wall, desk scene, phone or comparison sheet.

## Visual check

One clear subject, flat carved contours, coherent muted palette, actual print-language clouds/water, truthful source structure and cultural identity, quiet frame and whitespace, readable restrained title.
